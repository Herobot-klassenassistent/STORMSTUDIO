# The LinkedIn engine

An always-on system that turns the real work Kevin does into LinkedIn content that
lands jobs. Not a one-off campaign. A thread that keeps going: every appointment,
every finished product, every design decision is potential material, and we catch it
while it is fresh.

This folder is the home base. The weekly automation and this living queue keep it
alive even when Kevin is too busy on tour to write.

## Files
- `playbook.md` - the strategy: content pillars, what makes good material, format
  and photo rules, hashtags, and where to post (groups and the honest 2026 reality).
- `post-queue.md` - the living queue. Ready-to-paste posts at the top, a backlog of
  ideas below, and a posted log at the bottom. This is the file that grows.

## How the engine runs

1. **Kevin feeds it, lightly.** Any time something happens on the road, Kevin drops
   a one-line note into this chat thread: "just introduced the new leerlijn at school
   X", "finished the card set", "played Oradea tonight, thought about the teachers
   there". That is all I need. I turn it into a finished post with a photo suggestion
   and target groups.
2. **The automation stays alert.** Twice a week a routine wakes this same thread. I
   scan Gmail for new bookings, finished-work signals and warm replies, and scan the
   repo for newly built products, then draft one or two posts from whatever is
   genuinely new. If nothing new landed, I pull the strongest idea from the backlog so
   the queue never runs dry. I also look for gaps and bridges: a stage night and a
   school visit in the same week become one "two rooms, one craft" post.
3. **Kevin posts.** I only ever draft. Everything lands in `post-queue.md` ready to
   copy, with the language, the photo to attach, the first-comment link, and the
   groups and hashtags to use. Kevin posts from his phone and replies to comments.

## The rules (do not break)
- Draft only. Kevin posts everything himself from his own account.
- Voice and writing rules in `../SCHRIJFREGELS.md`: warm, first person, human, no em
  dashes, no smart quotes, no lists inside a post, clean visible links.
- The bio order is fixed (see `../gamification-course/MISSION.md`): primary teacher
  first, special education, twenty-plus years, music is the second career that made
  him go self-employed. Never "musician who fell into teaching".
- The special-needs lens leads. It is the thing almost no one else has.
- Honesty: never claim a product is finished or an event happened unless it did. If
  something is in progress, say so. "Building" is a great post; a fake launch is not.
