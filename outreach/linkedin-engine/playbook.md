# The LinkedIn engine playbook

How we turn Kevin's real work into posts that bring in jobs, assignments and
partnerships. Read this before writing any post.

## The core idea: build in public

Kevin is not selling in these posts. He is letting people watch him work. A teacher
and touring musician who designs curriculum, trains teams and builds classroom tools,
posting the process honestly, is magnetic to exactly the people who hire him: school
leaders, PD coordinators, culture and care organisations, edtech partners. The job
offers come from trust built over months of useful, human posts, not from a pitch.

Every post should do one of three jobs: teach something small and useful, show a real
moment from the work, or share a finished thing with pride. If a post does none of
those, it does not go out.

## The five content pillars

Rotate across these so the feed stays varied. Tag each queued post with its pillar.

1. **The craft of holding a room.** The bridge between stage and classroom. Engagement,
   attention, performance, why a lesson is a performance with a purpose. Kevin's
   signature territory and nobody else has this exact mix.
2. **Designing for the kid who checks out.** The special-needs-first method applied to
   any subject or product. Inclusion as better design, not extra work. The wedge.
3. **Building the products.** The gamification course, the AI class assistant, the card
   sets, curriculum and leerlijnen. Show the process, the decisions, the drafts, the
   fails. "Building in public."
4. **On the road.** The tour as a backdrop. Travel, venues, the double life, snatched
   moments of designing between soundchecks. This is what makes the feed his and not
   generic edtech.
5. **The work landing.** Appointments, school visits, trainings delivered, a team that
   "got it". Proud, specific, with a photo. These are the posts that most directly say
   "hire me".

## What makes a moment worth a post

When Kevin drops a note, I look for: a specific detail (a real kid, a real room, a real
problem solved), a feeling (pride, nerves, a small win), or a tension (two worlds
meeting, a thing that nearly failed). A booking confirmation, a finished draft, a warm
reply, a photo from a venue or a classroom: all of these are triggers. The gaps and
bridges between two pieces of work in the same week are often the best posts of all.

## Format rules

- Warm, first person, human. No em dashes, no smart quotes, no bullet lists inside the
  post. Short paragraphs, white space, one idea per paragraph.
- Open with a hook line that can stand alone, because LinkedIn truncates after a line
  or two. No "I am excited to announce".
- End most posts with a genuine question. Comments are where reach and conversations
  come from.
- Put any link in the FIRST COMMENT, never the post body. LinkedIn throttles posts with
  outbound links in the body.
- One post per weekday morning at most. Tuesday to Thursday, roughly 8 to 10am CET,
  pull best. Never more than one a day.
- Reply to every comment within a few hours. This matters more than the post itself.
- Length: 120 to 220 words is the sweet spot. A strong short post beats a padded one.

## Photos (Kevin is on tour, so keep it realistic)

A face or a real scene roughly doubles reach. Realistic sources while touring:
- On stage or at soundcheck (pillar 1 and 4).
- A laptop or notebook open on a tour bus, backstage, in a hotel: "designing between
  shows" (pillar 3 and 4).
- A classroom, workshop or team session when he does a school visit (pillar 2 and 5).
- A draft on screen, a page of a workbook, a sketch of a leerlijn, the card set in
  progress (pillar 3).
- An old photo from a past project when it fits (for example Oradea 2023).
A diptych of stage and classroom is the signature image and worth setting up once.
For each queued post I name the exact shot to attach. If Kevin has none, a clean
screenshot of the product or a simple quote-card still beats no image.

## Where to post, and the honest 2026 reality

The biggest lever is Kevin's own feed plus good hashtags plus tagging the right people
and organisations, then replying to every comment. LinkedIn groups reach far fewer
people than they did and most are low signal, so treat them as a small bonus, not the
plan. Still, a handful are worth cross-posting the most relevant pieces into.

**Hashtag sets (use three to five per post, mix big and niche):**
- Dutch education: #onderwijs #lesgeven #speciaalonderwijs #passendonderwijs
  #leraar #onderwijsinnovatie #curriculum
- International / edtech: #education #edtech #teaching #gamification
  #gamebasedlearning #AIineducation #inclusiveeducation #specialeducation
- Kevin's signature: #stormindeklas #buildinpublic (for the product posts)

**People and orgs to tag when genuinely relevant** (only when the post actually
relates to them, never spray): Delightex, MagicSchool, eduScrum / Willy Wijnands,
the LKCA, a school or organisation he just visited (ask them first for the proud
posts), and any co-presenter or host.

**LinkedIn groups worth trying (cross-post the fitting pieces, verify they are still
active before leaning on any):**
- Education Technology (edtech) and similar large global edtech groups.
- Gamification and Game-Based Learning groups.
- Special Education / Inclusive Education groups.
- Teachers Connect and other large teacher communities.
- Dutch: "Onderwijs" and "Leraren" oriented groups, and culture-education circles.
Verify each one is active and relevant before posting; a dead group is wasted effort.

**Beyond groups:** repost the strongest pieces to the StormStudio company page if one
exists, and consider the free guide as the thing links point to. The X / Bluesky plan
in `../gamification-course/market/x-strategy.md` is a parallel channel for the same
material when Kevin wants it.

## The weekly rhythm during the tour
Twice a week the automation drafts one or two posts into the chat thread and files them
in `post-queue.md`. Kevin skims on his phone, tweaks a word if he likes, posts the one
that fits the day, and drops a note back about anything new that happened. That note
becomes next week's material. The queue should always hold at least three ready posts.
