## How to use this

You have the drafts and the sell sheet going out. Interest is the easy part; the
booking happens in the reply. Keep this open. When someone answers, find the case
below that matches, paste, adjust the first line so it sounds like you, and send
within a few hours. Speed is most of the game.

One rule underneath all of it: always end with a single, easy next step. Never ask
"let me know if you're interested". Ask for a specific small yes, usually a short
call or a date.

## Case 1: "Interested, tell me more"

> Lovely to hear from you, and thank you for the quick reply.
>
> The simplest next step is a short call, twenty minutes, so I can hear about your
> group and your goals and tailor this properly rather than send you a brochure.
> Would some time next week suit? I'm fairly flexible around a touring schedule, so
> send me two or three moments that work for you and I'll make one fit.
>
> In the meantime I've attached a one-page overview so you can see the shape of it.
>
> Warm regards,
> Kevin

Attach the sell sheet. That's the whole move.

## Case 2: "Can you send details / a syllabus?"

> Of course, happy to.
>
> I've attached the one-page overview, and I can send the full course outline too.
> Before I do, one quick question so I send you the right version: is this for your
> own teachers as a team training, for a catalogue of open courses, or for an
> Erasmus+ group? Each one has a slightly different shape and I'd rather send you the
> one that fits.
>
> If it's easier, a fifteen-minute call sorts it in one go. Either way, tell me a
> little about the group and I'll come back with something concrete.
>
> Warm regards,
> Kevin

The question does two things: it sends the right thing, and it turns a document
request into a conversation.

## Case 3: "What does it cost?"

Price questions are buying signals. Answer plainly, then move to the call.

> Good question, and thank you for asking it directly.
>
> It depends a little on the format, so here's the honest range. A live training for
> one group, delivered at your school, runs between 2,400 and 3,600 euro depending on
> length and travel. Per participant on an Erasmus+ course it's roughly 320 to 400
> euro a seat. The self-paced download is 149 euro for one teacher or 549 for a
> school licence.
>
> Most schools fund the live training through their own professionalisation budget or
> through Erasmus+ KA1, and I'm glad to help you word the application if that helps.
>
> Shall we take fifteen minutes to match the format to your budget? Send me a couple
> of times and I'll confirm one.
>
> Warm regards,
> Kevin

## Case 4: "Not right now / maybe later"

Do not push. Leave the door open and the free thing in their hand.

> Completely understand, and thank you for being straight with me.
>
> Let me leave you with something useful rather than nothing. Here's a free one-page
> guide, the six things that make a lesson genuinely pull a class in. Use it with your
> team whenever you like, no strings.
>
> I'll check back in a few months in case the timing has changed. And if something
> shifts before then, you know where I am.
>
> Warm regards,
> Kevin

Attach the free 6 Levers one-pager. Add them to your list and note a date to return.

> If either of those is a gap worth closing, a short call would tell us quickly.
> Worth twenty minutes?

> Warm regards,
> Kevin

## Case 6: A provider or conference says yes

Move fast and make it effortless for them.

> Wonderful, I'm really glad. Thank you.
>
> To make this easy on you, tell me what you need from me and I'll turn it around
> quickly: a course description for your catalogue, a short and long bio, a photo,
> dates I can hold, or a proposal form filled in. Send me whatever your process needs
> and consider it done.
>
> I'm genuinely looking forward to working together.
>
> Warm regards,
> Kevin

## The follow-up (for silence)

Most bookings come from the second email, not the first. If there's no reply after
five or six working days, send this once. Reply on top of your original so the thread
is there.

> Hi [name], just floating this back to the top of your inbox in case it slipped by on
> a busy week. No pressure at all. If gamification and immersive learning is something
> your team might want this year, I'd still love a quick chat. And if the timing isn't
> right, a one-line "not now" is a perfectly kind answer and I'll happily check back
> later.
>
> Warm regards,
> Kevin

One follow-up. If it's still quiet, let it rest and return in a season. Chasing
harder than that costs you more than the booking is worth.

## Your first call, in one minute

When you get the call, you don't need a script, just a shape. Ask first, pitch second.

- Ask what they teach, who's in the room, and what's not working now.
- Listen for the pain: workload, engagement, the learners who get left behind.
- Then show how one part of the course meets exactly that, not the whole thing.
- Agree the format and a date before you hang up, even a provisional one.
- Send a one-line written summary the same day with the next step in it.

> [!key] Every reply gets an answer the same day, and every answer ends with one easy
> next step: a short call or a date. That single habit turns these funnels into
> bookings.
