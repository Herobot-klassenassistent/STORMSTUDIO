# Selling it beyond cold email: borrowed audiences and inbound

Cold email is low yield and slow. The shift: stop pitching strangers one by one, and
instead show up inside audiences that already exist, and let other people's platforms
do the distributing. Kevin's story (twenty years in special education, then a touring
musician who went self-employed to keep both) plus a genuinely good product is made
for this. Ordered by leverage.

## Tier 1: do these first

### 1. A free live webinar as the funnel (replaces cold email with inbound)
"Build a playable lesson in 45 minutes." Free, live, online, once a month. Promote it
on LinkedIn and in teacher communities. People opt in, you teach something genuinely
useful, and at the end you show the course. This is the single highest-converting move
for a course, and it turns strangers into warm, opted-in leads who came to you.
- First action: pick a date, set a free registration page (I can build it as a public
  page like the sales page), and seed it with two LinkedIn posts and a few community
  drops. Run of show and copy below.

### 2. Podcast guesting (borrow their audience, tell your story)
Your story is a producer's gift, and you are a performer. Being a guest puts you in
front of thousands of exactly-right listeners with zero cold pitching of the product.
- Real target shows (they take guests): **House of #EdTech** (Chris Nesi),
  **The EdTech Podcast** (Philippa Wraithmell), **AmpED to 11** (Brett Roer, Rebecca
  Bultsma), **AI in Education Podcast**, **EdTechnical**. Plus NL education podcasts.
- Angle, not a pitch: "The touring musician who spent twenty years teaching special
  needs, on how to build lessons that actually pull a room in." Story first, course
  never mentioned until they ask.
- First action: a one-paragraph guest pitch (below) sent to five shows. This is warm,
  not cold, because you are offering value (a great episode), not asking them to buy.

### 3. AI-edtech tools with huge teacher audiences (co-marketing / ambassador)
These companies have the exact audience you want and actively run ambassador and
community programs. Become a contributor, not a cold emailer.
- **MagicSchool AI** (roughly 8 million educator signups; has an Ambassador program
  and AI Educator Awards). **Curipod**, **Diffit**, **Brisk**, **SchoolAI**,
  **Eduaide**, **Nearpod/Genially** communities.
- The offer to them: "I run a course on gamified, inclusive lessons and I would love
  to give your community a free workshop / guest lesson / webinar." You bring them
  value and content; they put you in front of their teachers.
- First action: apply to the MagicSchool Ambassador program and pitch one free
  community webinar to two of these.

## Tier 2: let others sell it for you, or use built-in traffic

### 4. Turn on Gumroad affiliates and seed edu creators
Gumroad has a built-in affiliate feature. Give teacher-bloggers, edu-YouTubers and
newsletter authors a cut (say 30 to 40 percent) plus a free copy. They promote to
their lists and you only pay on a sale. Zero cold outreach on your side.
- First action: enable affiliates in Gumroad, set the percentage, and send ten
  creators a short "here is a free copy, here is your affiliate link" note.

### 5. Teacher marketplaces (built-in teacher traffic)
List the mini-courses and lesson kits where teachers already shop.
- **Teachers Pay Teachers** (global, English versions), **LessonUp** (NL/EU creator
  marketplace), **Twinkl** partner content, **Wakelet** collections as free lead
  magnets. Each listing is a storefront you do not have to drive traffic to.

### 6. Delightex, deeper than the partner email
Get featured in their resource hub and ambassador content, not just a partnership
reply. Their users are your buyers. (Warm thread already exists.)

## Tier 3: inbound that compounds while you tour

### 7. SEO cornerstone content + YouTube tutorials
A few strong pieces that rank for what teachers actually search: "AI lesson planning
for teachers", "gamification lesson ideas", "inclusive game-based learning". Short
YouTube tutorials ("an AI lesson in ten minutes", "build a game in Delightex") are
searchable forever and funnel to the free guide, then the course.

### 8. Communities (be useful, let the profile sell)
Show up where teachers gather and help, do not pitch. Facebook teacher groups (NL and
international, special-education, gamification-in-education), Bluesky #edusky, Reddit
r/Teachers and r/education, edtech Discords and Slacks, and NL WhatsApp teacher groups.

- **Vakbonden and member orgs** (AOb, CNV Onderwijs) for member PD.
- **Museums and science centers** that run teacher PD (Oyfo, NEMO) as co-delivery.
- **White-label** the course to an edtech company or PD provider who bundles it with

## Ready to use

### The free-webinar run of show (45 minutes)
1. Hook (5 min): show a boring worksheet, then the same lesson as a playable one.
2. Teach (25 min): the six levers, then build one small playable moment live with a
   little AI and a Delightex example. Everyone leaves with a real idea.
3. Inclusion (5 min): the one move that makes it work for the kid who checks out.
4. The offer (5 min): "If you want the whole method, the course is here," plus a
   webinar-only discount code and the free guide.
5. Q and A (5 min).

Promo post (LinkedIn / communities):
> I am running a free 45 minute session: build a playable lesson, live, from one of
> your own. A little AI to design it fast, a taste of game design to bring it alive,
> and the one move that makes it work for every learner. Bring a lesson you are tired
> of. Free, [date], link in the comments.
