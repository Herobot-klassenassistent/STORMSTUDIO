# Selling the course on X (Twitter)

## The honest take: yes, but as a secondary channel, and here is the catch

X still has a real education and edtech crowd: teachers under hashtags like
#edutwitter and #AIinEducation, edtech founders, and the AI-in-teaching scene. For a
teacher-training product with a free lead magnet, it can drive downloads and
awareness, especially through threads and by replying usefully to big accounts.

Two caveats, straight:
- The teacher crowd has thinned since 2023; many moved to Bluesky, Threads and
  Mastodon. Bluesky's #edusky is genuinely active. Worth claiming the same handle
  on Bluesky and cross-posting; it may out-perform X for teachers.
- LinkedIn is still the higher-ROI channel for B2B teacher training. X is additive,
  not a replacement.

## Starter posts (paste, one per slot)

1. Most "gamification" is just points and badges. Kids see through it in a week. What
   actually works is quieter: a clear goal, fast feedback, the right size challenge, a
   bit of choice, and a reason to care. Build those and you barely need the points.

2. The real test of any lesson is the kid who usually checks out. If it works for
   them, it works for everyone. Twenty years in special education taught me that, and
   it changed how I design everything.

3. AI did not replace my teaching. It gave me my evenings back. Fifteen minutes to
   build the worksheet and the two differentiated versions, then the saved hour goes
   into the actual teaching. That is the whole trick.

4. Ask a class to play a game and you get a fun afternoon. Ask them to build one and
   you get coding, storytelling, design and teamwork, and a room that will not go
   home. Delightex makes it doable without a computer lab.

5. Inclusion is not extra work bolted onto a lesson. It is just better design. An
   easier path, a non verbal way to succeed, a quieter option. The whole class
   benefits, not only the child you built it for.

6. I came to teaching the long way, through music. Twenty years later the stage still
   teaches me about the classroom. A lesson is a performance with a purpose: a hook,
   some tension, something to do, and nobody in the back row left invisible.

7. Free, no strings: the six levers of engagement, one page, the honest version of
   what makes a lesson pull a room in. Take it, use it with your team, pass it on.
   stormintheclass.gumroad.com/l/six-levers
